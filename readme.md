# [DOA Estimation] Weighted Spatial Covariance Matrix Estimation in MUSIC-Based Speech Source Delay Time Difference Estimation [with MATLAB Code]

This paper investigates the estimation of Delay-Time Difference (TDOA) in noisy and reverberant environments. Traditional methods for TDOA estimation, such as Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC), suffer from poor robustness in noisy and reverberant conditions due to distortion in the spatial covariance matrix (SCM). To address this issue, we propose a robust method for SCM estimation, termed Weighted Spatial Covariance Matrix (WSCM). In the WSCM estimation process, each time-frequency bin of the input signal is weighted by a time-frequency mask. Under ideal conditions, the mask value for non-speech time-frequency bins is zero, while that for speech time-frequency bins is one. In practice, the mask values range between 0 and 1, predicted by a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network trained on large amounts of simulated noise and reverberation data. The use of mask weights significantly mitigates the impact of low signal-to-noise ratio time-frequency bins on SCM estimation, thereby enhancing the robustness of the MUSIC algorithm. Simulation and real-world data experiments demonstrate that the use of WSCM significantly improves the robustness of the MUSIC algorithm.
Over the past few decades, the Generalized Cross-Correlation (GCC) method based on phase transformation has been widely used for time delay estimation [9]. However, its limitation lies in that errors are amplified in regions with low signal power. Another popular method is the Multiple Signal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm, which divides the spatial covariance matrix into a signal subspace and a noise subspace through feature value decomposition. Then, it estimates the time delay by searching for orthogonal steering vectors to the noise subspace. However, in scenarios with low signal-to-noise ratio and high reverberation, the spatial covariance matrix influenced by noise and reverberation dominates the time-frequency bins, affecting the estimation of time delays. To address this issue, existing research has proposed various methods to select reliable time-frequency bins dominated by direct sound. For example, Guo et al. [4] tracked the envelopes of speech, reverberation, and background noise to extract reliable regions, but the assumption of only one speech source during the de-mixing process limits its applicability in practical applications. In [11], a method combining noise floor tracking, starting point detection, and coherence test was proposed to robustly identify time-frequency bins dominated by a single source. However, due to the use of fixed thresholds, some noise time-frequency bins may be mistakenly selected in extremely low signal-to-noise ratio scenarios.
Inspired by the application of mask-based methods based on neural networks in beamforming [12,13,14], we propose a WSCM-MUSIC method (called WSCM-MUSIC) based on weighted spatial covariance matrix (WSCM), which achieves robust arrival time difference estimation through speech-dominant time-frequency bin selection by a mask predictor based on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM). Specifically, the mask predictor is trained with a large amount of simulated noise and reverberation data to cover as many scenarios as possible. The predicted mask values range from 0 to 1, indicating the likelihood that each time-frequency bin is dominated by speech. Then, for each estimated mask-enhanced time-frequency bin, the weighted spatial covariance matrix is calculated. In this way, the influence of noise and reverberation in the weighted spatial covariance matrix is significantly reduced. The MUSIC algorithm is applied to the weighted spatial covariance matrix of each time-frequency bin to obtain pseudo spectrum. Finally, by summing up the pseudo spectra of all time-frequency bins, peaks are sought from the summed pseudo spectra to estimate arrival time differences, overcoming the problem of spatial clutter blurring in high frequency bands. Experimental results in both simulation and real environments show that the proposed WSCM-MUSIC method outperforms GCC-PHAT [9], learning-based systems [16], MUSIC [10] and its variants [11] in terms of performance under low signal-to-noise ratio and high reverberation conditions.
Conclusion This paper proposes the WSCM-MUSIC method for robust arrival time difference estimation in highly challenging environments. In addressing the problem of robust arrival time difference estimation, this paper elaborates on corresponding solutions and evaluates and compares various systems based on generalized cross-correlation and spatial covariance matrices. Experimental results show that the proposed WSCM-MUSIC method performs well in high noise and reverberation environments. Compared to a previous time-frequency bin selection method combining noise floor tracking, start point detection, and coherence testing, the advantages and robustness of the long short-term memory based mask predictor are validated. Since the long short-term memory based mask predictor is trained only for a single speaker and does not utilize arrival time difference information, future work will consider multi-speaker mask predictors and investigate incorporating arrival time difference information into the training process of the mask predictor.
